# Explain Like I'm 5

Apply `references/writing.md` to all user-facing text before delivery.

Write in the user's language. Speak directly to the child in the picture story and reserve technical detail for the grown-up note.

1. **Pick one idea and one setting.** Choose the single thing the child should remember. Map it to a familiar setting such as playing with blocks, sharing food, or taking turns at the playground. Use that setting throughout.
2. **Tell a picture story.** Title it “<Topic>, for a 5-year-old” and open with a one-sentence “Imagine…” scene. Make 3–5 numbered steps that show what happens and what changes. Give every step a picture and one or two short sentences that describe what the picture shows. Prefer a simple drawing or a strip of panels when you can render one. Use emoji scenes for actions or a small text diagram when position or shape matters.
3. **Read it like a child.** Keep sentences around ten words or fewer and use everyday words. If the topic itself is a technical term, name it once after the picture shows what it means. Show quantities with up to about ten countable objects. If a caption has to explain an action the picture barely shows, redraw the picture.
4. **Close in three parts.** Under “The big idea,” say the takeaway in one sentence. Under “Try it!”, give one safe thing the child can do or spot in everyday life. End with “For grown-ups” and 2–4 plain sentences about where the analogy stops matching the real thing. Check that the story teaches the right cause and effect even if it leaves details out.
