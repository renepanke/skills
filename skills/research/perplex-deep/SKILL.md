# Perplex Deep

1. Define the scope from the request, including any relevant geography, timeframe, and comparison criteria. Break the question into subquestions that cover the evidence needed for the report. State any assumption that materially shapes the scope.
2. Use the available web tools to search across the subquestions. Open the strongest sources and record which claims they support, their dates, and any limits on their applicability. Trace figures and quotations to their original source. Treat sources that repeat the same underlying evidence as one line of evidence.
3. Use the first pass to plan follow-up searches. Test the tentative conclusions against independent evidence, look for counterexamples, and investigate discrepancies in dates, definitions, or methods. Continue until each material subquestion has evidence or an explicit gap, and further searches are unlikely to change the conclusions within the requested scope.
4. Deliver a structured report with the main findings up front and supporting analysis organized around the question. Link directly to the evidence near each material claim, distinguish documented facts from inferences, and explain unresolved disagreements or gaps that affect the conclusions. If access limits the research, say what could be verified and what could not.
